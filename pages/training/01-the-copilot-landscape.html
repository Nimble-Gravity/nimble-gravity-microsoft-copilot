The Copilot Landscape

You already have Copilot — this lesson is the map. One assistant shows up on three surfaces: Copilot Chat, Copilot inside the M365 apps, and the reasoning agents, Researcher and Analyst. Advanced use starts with knowing which surface you're on, what it's grounded in, and what it can actually see.

01 — One assistant, three surfaces

Chat, the apps, and the agents

Everything in this session lives on one of three surfaces: Copilot Chat, Copilot in the Microsoft 365 apps, and specialized agents such as Researcher and Analyst. They share the same brain and the same data rules — what changes is where the work happens and how much of it you hand over.

Copilot Chat

The conversation surface — in the Microsoft 365 Copilot app, Teams, and Edge. Ask questions, draft, summarize, and reference specific files, people, and meetings. With your license it's grounded in your work data, and it's where you launch the specialized agents. Module 1 makes you sharp here.

Copilot in the apps

Copilot working inside Excel, Outlook, Word, PowerPoint, and Teams — where finance actually lives. Since April 2026 it takes real multi-step actions in your documents: editing cells, restructuring drafts, building decks. Module 2 goes app by app.

The reasoning agents

Researcher and Analyst — agents you brief like a colleague and check back on. Researcher produces cited, multi-source reports; Analyst runs chain-of-thought data analysis with visible Python. Modules 3 and 4 are theirs.

💡
The shift this session is about

Most people use Copilot like a search box: one question, one answer, copy, paste. Advanced use means grounding it in the right sources, letting it act inside your documents, and delegating whole jobs to agents — then reviewing like the professional whose name goes on the output.

02 — Where answers come from

Grounding is a set of signals, not a switch

In the current Copilot Chat experience, don't think of grounding as a single Work-vs-Web toggle. Think of it as a set of controls and signals: Work IQ, your prompt, the files you attach or reference, the sources Copilot cites, and the agent you choose. Your job is to control the context and verify the sources — every time.

Set the context

Work IQ, prompt scope, and files

Work IQ helps Copilot draw on your Microsoft 365 context — emails, meetings, chats, files you already have permission to open — when it's relevant. Reference a specific file or thread with "/" or an attachment, and say explicitly in the prompt when you want public web information. Ask for the source you want; don't assume Copilot will guess it.

Use for: anything about our numbers, our files, our meetings, our people — named explicitly.
Verify the source

Sources cited, agent selected

Before you read the answer, check where it came from: inspect the citations, files, and source cards Copilot shows you. The agent you pick matters too — Copilot Chat, an app, or Researcher/Analyst each ground differently. Market conditions, carrier news, a competitor's earnings release — ask for public web sources explicitly when the question is about the world outside the tenant.

Use for: anything you'd otherwise search for — asked for, and cited, explicitly.
💡
New design note: Work IQ

You may see Work IQ in the new design. Work IQ helps Copilot use Microsoft 365 context and organizational signals when appropriate. Work IQ Off should not be presented as a simple replacement for Web mode. The safer teaching point: ask for the source you want, provide the source when possible, and verify the sources Copilot used. (Facilitator note: Work IQ influences use of Microsoft 365 context, but source verification and prompt clarity still matter — that habit is what this whole session is teaching.)

03 — The data rules

What Copilot can — and can't — see

Three facts settle most of the "is this safe?" questions a finance team asks. They're Microsoft's own commitments, current as of July 2026 — and the third one is a nuance most teams miss.

01
Your permissions are the boundary
Copilot only surfaces content you already have at least view access to. It respects identity, sharing, and Purview sensitivity labels. It cannot read another team's folder just because you asked nicely.
02
No training on our data
Prompts, responses, and Graph data are not used to train the foundation models — including the OpenAI and Anthropic models running under the hood. Your close commentary isn't teaching anyone's model.
03
Checkpoint: save agent results as governed artifacts
Copilot chat history is retained and eDiscovery-searchable via Purview. But Researcher and Analyst session content is not e-discoverable by default. The rule, not a suggestion: the moment a Researcher or Analyst output becomes real work product, save it into a governed file — a document in the right SharePoint library, not left sitting in the agent's session. You'll hit this checkpoint again in Module 3 and Module 4.
04 — Why this matters here

The finance workload this absorbs

Brown & Brown's finance org consolidates hundreds of decentralized profit centers into two segments, closes the books through the biggest integration in company history, and supports a deal engine that signed 43 acquisitions in 2025. That's exactly the document-heavy, deadline-driven work these tools are built for.

Close & consolidation

Variance narratives across profit centers, budget-vs-actuals commentary, fiduciary cash rec exceptions, organic-revenue schedules — repetitive analysis that ends in a written explanation, every month.

The deal engine

Data-room review, target research, earn-out modeling, purchase-accounting workpapers, integration questions — research-heavy work where Researcher and Analyst carry the first draft.

Carrier economics

Contingent-commission estimates that hinge on carrier loss ratios, supplemental tracking, float income analysis — estimate-driven numbers that need a documented story behind them.
