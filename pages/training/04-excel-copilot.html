Excel + Copilot

Excel is where the finance month actually happens — so this is the deep lesson of Module 2. Since the April 2026 GA, Copilot in Excel doesn't just suggest formulas: it plans multi-step edits, executes them, runs Python against your data, and attributes every cell it touched. This lesson covers the agentic workflow, the audit trail, and the finance skills library.

Copilot in the Apps · ~15 min read
01 — What changed

From suggestions to actions

Copilot's agentic capabilities in Excel hit GA on April 22, 2026. The practical difference: it now takes multi-step actions in your workbook — restructuring, calculating, formatting — instead of handing you a formula to paste. Two modes, one habit to build.

Chat — ask about the data

Question-and-answer against the open workbook: "which profit centers drove the June expense variance?" Nothing changes in the grid — it reads, reasons, and answers. Your starting mode when you're exploring a file you didn't build.

Edit — hand it the work

Copilot builds and modifies the workbook itself: variance columns, pivot-style summaries, conditional formats, whole schedule tabs. This is where the multi-step agentic behavior lives — and where the safeguards below matter.

Plan with Copilot

For bigger edits, Copilot outlines its intended changes before executing — a plan you approve or redirect. As it works, it shows its step-by-step reasoning, so you watch the approach, not just the result.

🗺️
Read the plan like a review note

The plan is your intervention point. If Copilot intends to overwrite a column you need, or summarize at the wrong grain, say so before it runs — "keep the original actuals column, add the variance next to it" — exactly as you'd redirect a junior teammate's approach before they build the wrong thing.

02 — The power move

Python in the grid

As of April–May 2026, Copilot writes and runs Python inside the Excel edit workflow. You never leave the workbook, and you never write the code — you describe the analysis, and Copilot handles the rest.

What it does

Forecasting, statistics, cleaning — on request

Ask for a forecast, a correlation, an outlier scan, or a messy-data cleanup, and Copilot writes the Python, runs it against your sheet, and lands the results in the grid. Analysis that used to mean exporting to a specialist now happens in the file you already have open.

Try: "Forecast Q3 commission income by profit center from the monthly actuals, and flag centers trending below budget."
Why finance cares

The judgment-heavy asks become one prompt

Seasonality in contingent-commission accruals, rate sensitivity on fiduciary-cash income, outlier detection across hundreds of profit-center P&Ls — statistical work that supports an estimate or a reviewer's question, without leaving the workbook or waiting on another team.

Microsoft's own framing: "Copilot in Excel: Built for the era of Frontier Finance" (June 25, 2026).
🔎
Before you trust the forecast — check what it touched

Python ran against your sheet, but that doesn't make the output right. Open Review → Show Changes and confirm the cells Copilot modified are the ones you expect, built from the range you actually pointed it at — before a forecast or outlier flag goes anywhere near a working paper.

03 — Auditability

Every cell it touched, on the record

For a controllership audience, this is the feature that makes agentic Excel usable at all: Copilot's edits are attributed in the Show Changes pane, cell by cell. The habit that goes with it — verify before you trust — is non-negotiable.

01
Show Changes attributes Copilot
Open Review → Show Changes after any Copilot edit. Every cell Copilot modified is attributed to it, alongside the human edit history — so a reviewer can always separate what the tool did from what you did.
02
Spot-check like a reviewer
Trace two or three of Copilot's numbers back to source cells before you rely on any of them. If a variance doesn't foot, tighten the prompt and rerun — the cost of a recheck is seconds; the cost of a wrong number in the close package is not.
03
Your name is still on the workbook
Attribution tells you what Copilot changed; it doesn't sign off for you. Treat Copilot's output exactly like a preparer's draft: reviewed, tied out, then owned. That review discipline is the whole reason this workflow survives an audit.
04 — Reusable leverage

The finance skills library and connectors

The newest layer, GA around July 2026: custom skills — reusable finance workflows saved as markdown files in OneDrive and invoked by @-mention — plus live financial data connectors. Microsoft ships a finance skills library out of the box; your best prompts can join it.

The out-of-the-box library

@variance-analysis, @model-update, @comps-analysis, @deal-screening, @portfolio-monitoring, @catalyst-calendar — Microsoft's own finance workflows, invoked by @-mention in the Copilot pane. @variance-analysis alone maps straight onto the monthly close.

Market data, connected

Financial data connectors bring FactSet, PitchBook, Morningstar, S&P Global, Daloopa, and CB Insights into the grid — carrier financials for contingent estimates, comps for deal screening — without the copy-paste from a terminal window.

Skills you write yourself

A custom skill is just a markdown file in OneDrive describing a workflow — the organic-revenue exclusion schedule, the earn-out remeasurement check. Write the steps once, and the whole team invokes them by @-mention instead of re-explaining the process every month.
