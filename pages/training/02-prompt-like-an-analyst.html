Prompt Like an Analyst

The gap between a mediocre Copilot answer and a great one is almost always the prompt. Microsoft's own framework has four elements — Goal, Context, Expectations, Source — and finance work rewards every one of them. This lesson drills the framework, the grounding moves, and the automation that runs your best prompts on a schedule.

Foundations & Copilot Chat · ~15 min read
01 — The framework

Four elements, every time

This is Microsoft's published prompt guidance, and it maps perfectly onto how finance already writes a good request to a junior teammate: what you need, why, what format, and from which source.

01
Goal — what you want
"Summarize the June variances over 10% by profit center." Specific, singular, verb-first. One goal per prompt — chain follow-ups rather than stacking asks.
02
Context — why, and for whom
"I'm preparing close commentary for the segment controller ahead of Thursday's review." Context changes what gets emphasized — audiences hear different variances.
03
Expectations — format and tone
"A table of the five largest variances with a one-sentence driver each, then a three-bullet summary in plain, neutral language." Say what good looks like; you'll get it.
04
Source — which files or data
"Use /profit-center-pnl.csv" — reference the file, meeting, or person directly. Source is the element people skip, and it's the one that stops Copilot guessing.
02 — Grounding moves

Point it at the right sources

Grounding is how you turn "write about variances" into "write about our variances." Two moves cover most of it — and Copilot Search and Memory quietly upgrade both.

Reference

Slash in files, people, and meetings

Type "/" in Copilot Chat to reference a specific file, email, meeting, or person — or attach files outright. "Summarize the open items from /Close calendar sync with what /Priya Sharma still owes" beats any paragraph of description.

If the answer must come from a specific document, name the document.
Scope

Pick Work or Web deliberately

Before you type, decide where the truth lives. Our numbers, our meetings → Work. Carrier news, market rates → Web. When you need both, that's a hint the job belongs to Researcher (Module 3), which can scope each run to work, web, or both.

Copilot Search finds the file first; Memory carries your preferences between chats.
03 — The upgrade in practice

Same ask, rebuilt with all four

Here's the difference on a real close task. The first prompt gets a generic essay. The second gets something you'd actually paste into the close package — try it yourself in the lab.

Before

"Explain the variances in our P&L." — No goal boundary, no audience, no format, no source. Copilot has to guess all four, and it will guess generic.

After

"Using /profit-center-pnl.csv, identify the five largest unfavorable June variances vs budget. I'm drafting close commentary for the segment controller. Give me a table — profit center, line, $ variance, likely driver — then three neutral summary bullets."

✍️
Say do, not don't

Positive instructions outperform prohibitions: "keep the tone neutral and factual" works better than "don't editorialize." And iterate — the second prompt in a thread, refining the first answer, is where the quality jumps. The Prompt Gallery in the Copilot app is a solid source of starting points.

04 — Automation

Your best prompts, on a schedule

Once a prompt earns its keep, stop retyping it. Scheduled prompts run any prompt on a recurrence — up to 10 per user — and drop the output into your Copilot chat history. For a finance calendar that repeats every month, this is free leverage.

01
Monday morning digest
"Summarize emails and meetings from last week tagged to the close calendar; list open items by owner." Runs before you're at your desk.
02
Close-week status pull
Daily during close week: "From my mail and chats since yesterday, list new issues raised on the consolidation, each with who raised it and status."
03
Recurring commentary refresh
Monthly, after the flash: your full four-element variance-commentary prompt, pointed at the updated file. Review and polish instead of starting cold.
