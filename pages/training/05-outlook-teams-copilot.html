Outlook & Teams

Close week generates its own weather system: status emails from dozens of profit centers, back-to-back review calls, and the same question answered five times. Copilot in Outlook and Teams doesn't make the close shorter — it takes the re-reading, re-typing, and minute-taking off your plate so the hours go to the numbers.

Copilot in the Apps · ~10 min read
01 — Outlook

The close-week inbox, triaged

The basics — summarize a long thread, draft a reply from instructions — you may already use. The 2026 agentic experiences are the upgrade: Copilot works your inbox and calendar, not just one message at a time.

01
Summarize and draft, grounded
Summarize a 30-message thread on an intercompany issue in seconds; draft the reply from instructions — "confirm the entry posts Thursday, ask who owns the recurring fix." The four-element framework from Module 1 applies verbatim.
02
Inbox triage and prioritization
The 2026 agentic experience: Copilot triages and prioritizes the inbox itself — surfacing what actually needs you first. On day three of close, "which of these 80 emails block the consolidation?" is the question that matters.
03
Conflicts and agendas, handled
Copilot reschedules calendar conflicts and builds meeting agendas straight from the chat — "set up a 30-minute earn-out review Thursday, agenda from this thread." The logistics of close week stop being your job.
04
Rewrite just the passage
Select one paragraph of a drafted email and have Copilot rewrite only that — sharper, shorter, more neutral — leaving the rest untouched. Precision editing for messages where tone matters, like anything going to a profit-center leader.
📥
Before you send — check what the summary left out

A triaged inbox and a drafted reply both lean on Copilot having read the thread correctly. Before you hit send, skim the source messages it summarized — a 30-message thread compressed to three sentences can lose a caveat along with the noise.

02 — Teams

Nobody takes minutes anymore

Every close call, deal review, and profit-center check-in can end with notes, owners, and action items already written. Two capabilities carry it: intelligent recap after the meeting, and the Facilitator agent during it.

Intelligent recap

Notes, speakers, and action items — after every call

Recorded or transcribed meetings get an automatic recap: notes with speaker attribution, action items, and the ability to ask questions of the meeting afterward. Ask for a custom summary in executive report format and the recap becomes the status update you'd otherwise write by hand.

During the meeting, Copilot answers "what did I miss?" and "where do we disagree?" live — useful when you join the second half of a review.
Facilitator agent

Real-time notes with owners, as you talk

The Facilitator agent (GA November 2025) takes shared notes in real time during the meeting — decisions and action items with owners, visible to everyone as they're captured. The close call ends and the follow-up list already exists, agreed on the spot instead of reconstructed the next morning.

Correct the notes live — "that one's on the Retail segment team, not corporate" — and the record is right before anyone leaves.
🗒️
Before you circulate the recap — check the attribution

Recap and Facilitator notes carry speaker attribution and named owners for a reason: check them before the notes go out. An action item attributed to the wrong profit-center lead is a correction nobody wants to chase down after close.

03 — A worked finance rhythm

Close week, with nobody re-typing status

The pieces compound when a team runs them as a system. Here's a close-week rhythm that combines recap, triage, and the scheduled prompts from Module 1 — the status information flows; nobody transcribes it.

01
Every close call ends written up
Daily close stand-ups run with Facilitator taking real-time notes; longer reviews rely on recap with an executive-format summary. Decisions and owners are captured at the moment they're made — once.
02
Each morning starts triaged
First move of the day: Copilot triages the overnight inbox against one question — "what blocks the consolidation today?" Blocking items get drafted replies; the rest waits until the flash is out.
03
A scheduled prompt writes the digest
A daily scheduled prompt sweeps mail, chats, and meeting recaps since yesterday into one close-status digest: open items, owners, new issues. The controller reads one summary instead of chasing five threads.
04
Status meetings shrink
Because the digest already says where everything stands, the stand-up spends its minutes on judgment calls — the earn-out remeasurement, the contingent estimate — not on reading status around the room.
🔁
One person sets it up; the whole team stops re-typing

The digest prompt is a one-time build — write it with all four elements, schedule it daily for close week, and share the prompt text with the team. Ten minutes of setup buys back the "any update on…?" messages for every close that follows.
