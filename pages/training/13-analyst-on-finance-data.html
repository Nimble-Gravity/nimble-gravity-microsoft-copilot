Analyst on Finance Data

The four-element prompt framework still applies — but analysis work adds a discipline of its own: name the metric, the dimension, and the time grain, and Analyst computes what you meant instead of what it guessed. This lesson drills that pattern, then hands you three worked runs on the workshop's synthetic finance files — variance trends, contingents at risk, and a stated-assumptions forecast.

The Analyst Agent & Capstone · ~15 min read
01 — The analysis brief

Name the metric, dimension, and grain

"Compare X by Y at Z grain, highlight trends" — that sentence shape is the whole pattern. Every reliable Analyst run names all three; every disappointing one left at least one of them for the agent to guess.

01
Metric — what to measure
"Compensation & benefits variance vs budget in dollars and as % of budget" — not "costs." Name the column, the calculation, and the units. If a metric is derived, define it in the prompt.
02
Dimension — sliced by what
By profit center, by carrier, by segment. The dimension decides what the answer's rows are. Finance questions usually carry two — say both, and say which one leads.
03
Grain — at what time step
Monthly January–June, YTD through June, quarterly. Grain is the element people skip, and it's why "trend" answers come back blended. Persistent-vs-one-off questions are grain questions.
📐
Clean tabular data in, reliable analysis out

Analyst is only as good as the table you hand it: headers in the first row, one row per observation, one table per sheet, consistent units. The workshop CSVs are already in that shape — when you move to your own extracts, thirty seconds of tidying beats three rounds of corrections.

02 — Three worked patterns

Run these on the workshop data

Three copy-ready briefs against the synthetic lab files in your OneDrive — fake data, shaped like ours, safe to upload. Each maps to a real desk task: multi-month variance trend, contingent accruals at risk, and a simple forecast with stated assumptions. Watch the Python pane on every run.

Pattern A — variance trend · profit-center-pnl.csv 
Using profit-center-pnl.csv: which profit centers show persistent unfavorable variances vs budget in Compensation & benefits from January through June 2026 — unfavorable in at least four of the six months? I'm drafting integration-period expense commentary. Quantify each one monthly, in dollars and as % of budget, in a table sorted by cumulative unfavorable variance, and chart the monthly trend for the top three.
Pattern B — contingents at risk · carrier-commission-statements.csv 
Using carrier-commission-statements.csv: for each carrier, compare the June YTD loss ratio to that carrier's contingent profit-share threshold. Which carriers' contingent accruals are at risk — loss ratio above, or within five points of, the threshold? I'm supporting the contingent-commission estimate for the quarter. Give me a table with YTD contingent accrual booked, YTD loss ratio, threshold, and headroom per carrier, then chart loss ratio against threshold by carrier.
Pattern C — simple forecast · profit-center-pnl.csv 
Using profit-center-pnl.csv: build a simple forecast of Core commissions by segment (Retail and Specialty Distribution) for July through December 2026, based on the January–June 2026 actuals. Use a straightforward trend method, state every assumption explicitly before the numbers, show the fitted trend against the actuals, and give me a monthly forecast table by segment plus one chart. Note where the two segments' run rates diverge — neutral language, no narrative beyond the data.
🎯
Notice what each brief names

Metric ("unfavorable variance vs budget, $ and %"), dimension ("by profit center", "by carrier", "by segment"), grain ("monthly, January–June", "YTD through June"). Plus the Module 1 elements: context for the audience, expectations for the format. That's the whole craft — the rest is verification.

03 — The verify habit

Read the Python before you trust the number

Analyst shows you its method as it runs — that's not decoration, it's the review step. Three moves turn a plausible-looking output into a figure you'd put your name on.

Check the filters and the sums

In the code: did it filter the months, accounts, and segments you meant? Did it sum the column you meant, with the sign convention you meant — is "unfavorable" your definition or its own? Two minutes of reading beats an hour of downstream cleanup.

Re-ask with corrections

The first output is a draft. "Redo that, but treat unfavorable expense variance as actual above budget, and exclude the profit center acquired mid-period from the trend" — corrections in-thread are cheap, and they sharpen the method on record.

Export into governed files

Agent session content isn't e-discoverable by default — so anything that feeds a close package or a memo gets saved out of the chat and into a governed OneDrive or SharePoint file. The analysis isn't done until it lives somewhere the record-keeping reaches.
