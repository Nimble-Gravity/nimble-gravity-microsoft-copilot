Make the two hours stick

The session is two hours; the habit is built in the two weeks after. Your teammates take their cue on a change like this from you, not from a portal — and after a single session, what you do in week one decides whether advanced Copilot use becomes a team habit or a memory. Five actions, a script, and the two hard questions.

For managers · ~5 min read
Why you

Your team reads you

Change-management research is unambiguous: teammates prefer to hear about a change like this from their immediate manager, and active manager support is one of the top predictors of adoption. With a one-session format there's no second session to catch stragglers — the reinforcement is you, in week one.

What to do

Five actions in the two weeks after

01
Protect practice time in week one
The take-homes — The Close Room team drill and the one-week practice plan — need real calendar time. Block it in week one and mean it; practice deferred past the close cycle is practice that never happens.
02
Ask for the artifacts in 1:1s
Don't ask "are you using Copilot?" — ask to see the practice-plan artifacts: the variance table, the Researcher report, the Close Room one-pager. Asking for the output signals it matters and surfaces where people are stuck.
03
Route wins into the prompt library
When a teammate lands a prompt that works — a variance-commentary pattern, a data-room brief — get it into the team's shared prompt library alongside the cheat sheet. One person's win becomes everyone's starting point.
04
Model scheduled prompts yourself
Set up one scheduled prompt of your own — a Monday close-status digest, a weekly variance summary — and mention it in the team meeting. Nothing normalizes a tool faster than watching the manager run on it.
05
Name a champion
Pick the teammate who took to it fastest and name them the team's Copilot champion — first stop for questions, curator of the prompt library, your early-warning system on what's working and what isn't.
If you want a script

What to say to your team

You don't need this — but if a 30-second version helps, here it is.

🗣️
30-second version

"We're doing one two-hour hands-on session on advanced Copilot — the Excel deep dive, the Researcher and Analyst agents, the whole toolkit we're already licensed for. The point is capacity: we're absorbing the biggest integration in company history, and this is how we take the drafting load off so your time goes to the judgment calls. Do the 15-minute pre-work, bring your laptop, and in the week after I'll be asking to see what you built. There's one ground rule on data — read it first."

The questions you'll get

Say this / don't say this

Two questions come up every time: "will this replace roles?" and "is our data safe in it?" Don't dodge either — the honest answers are also the reassuring ones.

Say this

Capacity, judgment, and the data facts

On roles: "Copilot drafts; you review, decide, and sign off — the judgment and the accountability stay with you. This is capacity for the integration workload, and verification is the skill we're investing in." On data: "It only sees what you already have permission to open, and Microsoft doesn't use our prompts or files to train the models. The Rules of the Road cover the rest."

Teammates who learn to direct and verify it well become more valuable, not less.
Don't say this

Avoid the efficiency framing — and the hand-wave

Skip "do more with fewer people" — it's the fastest way to kill adoption and drive quiet non-use. And don't wave off the data question with "it's fine, it's Microsoft"; give the two concrete facts (permissions-bound, no training on our data) and point to the Rules of the Road for the boundaries.

If you're unsure how to answer something, point them to the FAQ rather than improvising.
