Copilot Quick Reference

Everything worth remembering on one page — the surface map, the four prompt elements, the Excel moves, how to brief the agents, scheduled prompts, and the data-hygiene rules.

Print-friendly · keep it handy
01 — The surface map

Which Copilot am I on?

One assistant, three surfaces. Advanced use starts with picking the surface deliberately.

01
Copilot Chat — Work scope
Grounded in your emails, meetings, chats, and files via Microsoft Graph. Use for anything about our numbers, our files, our people.
02
Copilot Chat — Web scope
Grounded in live web results. Use for market conditions, carrier news, the rate environment — anything you'd otherwise search for.
03
Copilot in the apps
Inside Excel, Outlook, Word, PowerPoint, Teams — it acts directly in the open document. Use when the deliverable is the file you're already in.
04
The agents
Researcher and Analyst — brief them like a colleague and check back later. Use when the job is a whole analysis or report, not a single answer.
02 — Prompting

The four elements

Microsoft's official framework. Every serious prompt carries all four — and the three habits below keep them working.

01
Goal · Context · Expectations · Source
What you want, why and for whom, what good output looks like (format, length, tone), and where to look. Miss one and you'll iterate twice as long.
02
Say do, not don't
Positive instructions land better. "Use plain, factual close-package language" beats "don't be flowery."
03
One goal per prompt
Find, then explain, then draft — three prompts in one thread, not one prompt doing three jobs. Iterate instead of restarting.
04
Reference with "/"
Type "/" to reference specific files, people, and meetings — or attach the file. Grounded beats generic every time.
03 — Excel

The Excel moves

The deepest Copilot for finance work. Three habits: plan, analyze in Python, verify every touched cell.

01
Plan with Copilot first
For multi-step changes, ask Copilot to outline what it intends to do before executing. Review the plan, then approve — same discipline as reviewing a junior's approach.
02
Ask for Python analysis
"Analyze [range] with Python: variance by profit center, largest first" — Copilot writes and runs Python in the grid for forecasting, statistics, and heavy transforms.
03
Verify with Show Changes
Show Changes attributes every cell Copilot touched. Check it before you trust an edited workbook — that attribution trail is your audit answer.
04 — Agents

Briefing Researcher & Analyst

They share one budget: 25 combined queries per person per month, resetting on the 1st. One well-briefed run beats three vague ones.

01
Researcher — brief like a memo
State who you are, the sources (work files, web, or both), the questions to answer, and the output format. It produces a structured, cited report.
02
Answer the clarifying questions
Researcher asks before it runs — engaging with that phase is the #1 best practice. Then walk away: simple runs finish in under 5 minutes, complex ones take 10–45.
03
Commission early
Because runs take time, kick off the Researcher run first and keep working. Check back when it's done — never sit and watch it.
04
Analyst — name the shape
Name the metric, the dimension, and the time grain ("EBITDAC margin by profit center by month"). Supply clean tabular data — multiple files at once is fine.
05
Read the Python
Analyst shows its Python live. Skim it to verify the method — did it filter, sum, and group the way you would have? That pane is your review step.
05 — Scheduled prompts

Put the recurring asks on a schedule

Any prompt can run on a schedule — up to 10 per person — with output landing in your chat history. Works in Copilot Chat, Teams, and Outlook.

01
Monday digest
"Every Monday 8 AM: summarize last week's emails and meetings about [topic], flag anything awaiting my response." Start-of-week triage, done before coffee.
02
Close-week status
Daily during close week: "Summarize today's messages in the close channel and list open items by owner." Retire it when close ends.
03
Commentary refresh
Monthly, after the data lands: "Using /[file], draft the variance-commentary paragraph in our standard format." The recurring narrative, pre-drafted.
04
Mind the cap
Ten scheduled prompts per person. Review yours monthly and retire the ones you've stopped reading — a digest nobody opens is just noise.
06 — Data hygiene

The rules that keep this safe

Five facts and habits that settle most "is this okay?" questions. Current as of July 2026.

01
Permissions are the boundary
Copilot only sees content you already have access to. It honors identity, sharing, and sensitivity labels — it can't read another team's folder because you asked nicely.
02
No training on our data
Prompts, responses, and Graph data are not used to train the foundation models. Your close commentary isn't teaching anyone's model.
03
Save agent outputs to governed files
Researcher and Analyst session content is not e-discoverable by default. Anything that matters gets saved into a file in a governed location — that's the record.
04
Pick Work vs Web deliberately
Check the scope before every serious question. Work for our data, Web for the world outside the tenant — and scope agent runs the same way.
05
Synthetic data only in labs
Workshop exercises run on the synthetic files from the resource library. Never put real client, carrier, or deal data into a practice prompt.
