# Project Lighthouse — Proposed Earn-Out Term Sheet (Seller Draft)

> **Workshop material — entirely fictional.** Safe to upload to Copilot in the lab.

Extract of the seller's proposed consideration structure, as received. The deal team has
**not** accepted these terms; finance is asked to model and critique them.

## Consideration

- **Closing payment:** $118,000,000 cash, debt-free / cash-free, subject to a net working
  capital peg to be set from a 12-month average.
- **Earn-out:** for each of the **three** 12-month periods following close, seller receives
  **6.0× the amount by which Organic Revenue for that period exceeds the prior period's
  Organic Revenue**. **No cap.**

## Definitions (seller draft)

- **"Organic Revenue"** means all commissions and fees of the Business, *including
  profit-sharing contingent commissions and supplemental commissions*, excluding revenue
  attributable to books of business acquired by the Business after close.
- Measurement periods run from the first day of the month following close.
- Earn-out amounts are payable in cash within 60 days of each measurement date.

## Finance review questions (for the lab)

1. The definition **includes contingent commissions** in Organic Revenue. Given the revenue
   mix in `target-financials.csv` and the concentration facts in
   `carrier-contract-summary.md`, what behavior does this reward, and what happens to the
   earn-out in a benign-weather year followed by a catastrophe year?
2. The earn-out is **uncapped** and multiplies *incremental* revenue by 6.0×. Model the
   payment if contingents repeat their 2023→2025 trajectory ($2.45M → $4.9M) for one more
   period. How does that compare to the value actually created?
3. How would Brown & Brown's own quarterly **Level-3 fair-value remeasurement** of this
   earn-out behave under these terms — what inputs would move it, and how much P&L noise
   should the close team expect?
4. Draft the counter-structure finance would recommend (cap, collar, contingents carve-out,
   or an EBITDA-based measure), with a one-paragraph rationale for the deal team.
